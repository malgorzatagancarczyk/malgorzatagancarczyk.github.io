**English title:** <i>Narrative Strategy and the Illusion of Self-Interpretation in Łukasz Orbitowski’s Kult</i> (in Polish)

Supervisor: dr Michalina Kmiecik · Faculty of Polish Studies, Jagiellonian University, 2020

The thesis reads <i>Kult</i> (2019) alongside other quasi-dialogic novels — Wiesław Myśliwski’s <i>Traktat o łuskaniu fasoli</i> and John Crowley’s <i>Engine Summer</i> — in which a single narrator speaks to a silent listener and builds his own identity in the process. Using Mieke Bal’s narratology (focalization, primary and embedded text), Piotr Kubiński’s concept of emersion and Paul Ricoeur’s narrative identity, it shows how the narrator, Zbyszek, shapes his self-image: through affective descriptions of space, stereotyping of the Other (above all women and “true masculinity”) and manipulation of chronology. The story he tells is less about his brother, the visionary, than about himself — and it shows how narrative not only creates identity, but just as often imposes it.
