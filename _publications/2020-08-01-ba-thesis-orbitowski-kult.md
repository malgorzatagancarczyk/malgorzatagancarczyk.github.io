**English title:** <i>Narrative Strategy and the Illusion of Self-Interpretation in Łukasz Orbitowski’s Kult</i> (thesis in Polish)

Supervisor: dr Michalina Kmiecik · Faculty of Polish Studies, Jagiellonian University, 2020

## Abstract

The thesis examines the narrative strategy of Łukasz Orbitowski’s novel <i>Kult</i> (2019) and the illusory nature of the narrator’s self-interpretation. Like Wiesław Myśliwski’s <i>Traktat o łuskaniu fasoli</i> and John Crowley’s <i>Engine Summer</i>, <i>Kult</i> takes the form of a quasi-dialogue: a single narrator, Zbyszek, tells his story to a silent listener, and the novel divides into a primary text (the 1997 flood) and an embedded text (the Marian apparitions in Oława and the fate of his brother, the visionary). Drawing on Mieke Bal’s narratology, Piotr Kubiński’s concept of emersion and Paul Ricoeur’s narrative identity, the thesis analyses how the narrator constructs his own identity through affective descriptions of space, stereotyping of the Other — above all women and “true masculinity” — and manipulation of chronology. It argues that Zbyszek’s story is less about his brother than about himself, and that narrative not only creates identity but just as often imposes it.

**Keywords:** narratology, focalization, narrative identity, emersion, Łukasz Orbitowski, contemporary Polish fiction
